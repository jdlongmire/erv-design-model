# ERV Design Model

**The question:** Can the viral sequences embedded in human and ape genomes be explained as well by a design model as by common descent, and what evidence would decide between them?

This repository is a public research programme working on that question. It studies endogenous retroviruses (ERVs), the remains of ancient retrovirus infections that became part of the genome and are passed down through generations, and asks whether an originally designed, functionally mature biosphere could account for a meaningful subset of the patterns they form today.

**Status, in plain words:** Early stage. We think this model is worth testing; we do not claim it explains the evidence better than the standard explanation. On the most precise evidence available today, matching insertions at identical genomic locations in different species, the standard explanation is currently ahead. This work has not been peer reviewed.

**What would count against the model:** If insertions shared at the same genomic location, with matching boundaries and shared disabling changes, keep requiring shared ancestry, and the design model can offer no independently testable alternative, then the model fails on that evidence. The programme is set up to record that outcome if it comes, not to explain it away.

**What we assume, and what we test:** The programme starts from design premises, including theological ones. Those premises are stated openly in the charter and are not findings. What the evidence is meant to test is narrower: whether a model built on those premises can account for the ERV patterns as well as the standard explanation can.

**Approach:** This programme is design-forward. It begins with the explanatory plausibility of design, and it examines the consensus explanation critically: whether its mechanisms are causally sufficient for the patterns claimed, and whether its historical assumptions are independently supported or only fitted to the data. No account here earns credit for being the consensus. Competing accounts are judged on what they actually explain, how their registered predictions perform, and whether their assumptions are independently justified. The charter states this stance in full.

## How the programme is organized

The programme follows the research programme method of Imre Lakatos, with the parts renamed in plain words:

- **Core commitments** (the hard core): what the programme assumes and does not test directly. Listed in the charter.
- **Working models** (the protective belt): the parts we expect to revise as evidence comes in, such as how insertions were inherited, moved, recruited for new functions, or lost.
- **Research plan** (the positive heuristic): the order in which tests are run, set out in advance.
- **Rules against special pleading** (the negative heuristic): a failed prediction may not be rescued by an adjustment invented only to save it. Any rescue must be testable in its own right and registered before a new test.
- **Gaining ground or losing ground** (progressive or degenerating): the programme is gaining ground when a revision predicts something new, registered in advance, and the prediction holds up. It is losing ground when revisions only explain what is already known, or registered predictions keep failing. If the record shows the programme losing ground, it will be labelled as such here.

Words used in this repository are defined in plain language in [GLOSSARY.md](GLOSSARY.md).

## What is in this repository

- `PROGRAMME-CHARTER.md` - the programme's commitments, working rules, and the conditions under which it would be judged to have failed.
- `research/` - the current papers: the main whitepaper, a paper on functional genomic architecture, the causal model, a side by side comparison with the standard explanation, an evidence matrix, and audit records R1 to R8. Earlier drafts are kept separately in `research/history/`.
- `predictions/` - the place where predictions are written down and dated before the relevant data is examined, and where results, pass or fail, are logged.
- `methods/` - how the genome checks are run so that someone else can repeat them.
- `tools/` - the screening and checking programs used by the audits.
- `governance/` - the programme's internal records: evidence and citation rules, where each file came from, and a dated history of changes.

## The wider framework

This programme applies a broader framework, Designed Functional Maturity (DFM): the view that the first living systems were functionally complete rather than assembled gradually. The framework programme, covering origins questions well beyond viruses, is maintained at [https://github.com/jdlongmire/designed-functional-maturity](https://github.com/jdlongmire/designed-functional-maturity). This repository is that framework's primary public programme for endogenous retrovirus research. The framework repository's own ERV work package (WP-038) is its internal record; this programme's charter, predictions, and results are its own.

## Where the material came from, and what is not here

- The research here was drawn from the Original Biological Network work in the repository now named `jdlongmire/ztype-erv-explanatory-model`, published there by merge PR #3 (merge commit a3aaa8436d991f7fcefb0927581ca811fe1e5ce9). A file by file record of what came from where is in `governance/source-provenance-ledger.md`.
- This programme stands on its own. Two related research efforts run separately, and their findings, predictions, and verdicts are not used here as evidence for this model.
- The claims in the papers rest on the primary publications they cite. Where a source could not be checked directly, the file says so, and that material should not be treated as inspected data.
- No specific shared insertion is claimed as confirmed where the record does not show one. Similarity between viral families is not the same thing as the same insertion at the same genomic location, and this programme does not treat it as such.
