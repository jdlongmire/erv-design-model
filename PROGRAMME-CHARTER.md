# Programme Charter: ERV Design Model

**Established:** 2026-11-18
**Framework:** Designed Functional Maturity, the view that the first living systems were created functionally complete rather than assembled gradually
**Framework repository:** Designed Functional Maturity (DFM) is a broader research programme maintained at [https://github.com/jdlongmire/designed-functional-maturity](https://github.com/jdlongmire/designed-functional-maturity). This programme applies that framework to endogenous retrovirus evidence.
**Structure:** a research programme in the sense of Imre Lakatos, organized as core commitments, working models, a research plan, and rules against special pleading (see section headings)
**Status, in plain words:** Early stage. The model is, at most, preliminarily plausible. It does not claim to match the standard explanation yet, let alone beat it, and nothing here is historically confirmed.

Human-Curated, AI-Enabled (HCAE)

## 1. Purpose

To develop and test, in public and under dated commitments made in advance, whether a design model of endogenous retrovirus (ERV) evidence can become a programme that gains ground over time, and to record honestly if it does not.

### Programme stance (adopted 2026-11-18)

This programme is design-forward. It begins with the explanatory plausibility of design. The design model is the account under development here, not a challenger that must first displace an incumbent before it may be taken seriously. The consensus account, common descent, is a competing explanation, and its claims are open to the same critical examination as any other account's.

In practice:

- Consensus explanations are interrogated on two points: causal sufficiency (whether the mechanisms they invoke can actually produce the patterns claimed for them) and historical assumptions (whether the ancestral histories they assume are independently supported, or only fitted to the data they are meant to explain).
- No account earns credit for being the consensus, for incumbency, or for narrative familiarity. Overclaims of explanatory success, by any side, are recorded as overclaims.
- Competing accounts, this programme's included, are evaluated on three measures: demonstrated explanatory reach (what an account actually accounts for, not what it could accommodate after the fact), predictive performance on tests registered in advance, and independently justified assumptions (assumptions with support beyond the pattern they are invoked to explain).
- Findings stand as recorded. A result that favours a competing account on a specified body of evidence is an accounting result on that evidence. It is recorded plainly, and it does not convert the competing account into a default or a baseline.
- Design-forward is not contra-evidence. Facing the consensus account's evidence honestly does not weaken this programme's approach, and the programme must not make unsupported or false claims about the consensus account. What that account holds, predicts, or has demonstrated is stated accurately and with sources; the strongest form of its case is the one answered; and where its evidence is strong, the record says so. Caricature, gap-claims the literature has closed, and predictions attributed to it that it does not make are failures of this programme's own standard, on a par with the overclaims it records in others. The same rule binds claims made for the design model.

## 2. Core commitments (the hard core)

These are the programme's organizing commitments. They are held as starting assumptions, not as experimentally established facts:

1. Designed Functional Maturity: the initial biosphere was functionally mature, with working genomic, organismal, and ecological systems.
2. A possible original biological network: biological information handling that was functional from the start, potentially including beneficial viral or virus-like delivery systems and a functional genomic architecture.
3. Original lineages: there were original lineages whose boundaries, if the programme is to be tested empirically, must be specified independently of the ERV patterns under test.

Theological premises inside these commitments are premises. They must never be reported as measurements, sequence findings, or conclusions of a genomic analysis.

## 3. Working models (the protective belt)

The following are open to revision. Revising them is normal programme work, not a programme failure, provided each revision is recorded, dated, and counted as a change to the model's supporting assumptions:

- Causal models of inheritance, ERV integration, and viral transmission.
- Models of how an inserted element is later recruited for a function in the host (co-option) and of regulatory architecture.
- Models of genomic modification, silencing, degradation, and loss.
- Candidate lineage (kind) boundaries and host compatibility rules, when independently specified.
- Integration targeting, host range, and timing proposals.

No working model may be assigned arbitrarily to every observed location in the genome. An original placement, a loss, or a transfer invoked for a specific location requires an independently stated expectation or evidence specific to that location.

## 4. Research plan (the positive heuristic)

Work in this order, prospectively:

1. Comparative genomics at preselected locations, checking orthology (the same insertion at the same location in different species) in a way that takes genome assembly differences into account: both flanking regions, insertion boundaries, target site duplications, long terminal repeat (LTR) structure, and shared derived changes.
2. Independent lineage constraints: specify lineage boundaries and module expectations before selecting candidate locations.
3. Prospective location tests: use the template in `predictions/`; write down the prediction, what would count against it, and the scoring rule before inspecting the outcome.
4. Functional evidence: weigh demonstrated causal function separately from historical origin. A present function does not date an origin.
5. Quantitative comparisons between models: compare against the strongest common descent and exaptation models, and charge every model the same cost for the extra assumptions it needs. No numerical ranking without calibrated prior probabilities, sampling, and likelihood functions.

The starting test set is the five predeclared test families (T1 to T5) in the curated comparative evaluation: initial module specification, exact location and family tree agreement, nonorthologous transfer histories, symmetric screening in both directions across lineages, and functional system integration.

## 5. Rules against special pleading (the negative heuristic)

- Do not aim difficulties at the core commitments as a first move; test the working models first, in the open.
- Do not rescue a failed prediction with an original placement invented for the occasion, unrestricted loss, or an undocumented transfer. A rescue that is not independently testable, and registered in advance of a new test, is a losing-ground move and must be logged as one.
- Do not misrepresent evidence: do not present family similarity as the same insertion at the same location, a gap in a genome assembly as an absence, an unchecked source as inspected data, or a premise as a finding.

## 6. Gaining ground or losing ground (progressive or degenerating problemshift)

The programme is assessed on a dated record, in `predictions/result-log.md` and `governance/change-history.md`:

- Gaining ground: a revision predicts a new fact, registered before the test, and at least some such predictions hold up under the stated scoring rule.
- Losing ground: revisions only explain facts already known, extra assumptions multiply without being tested, or registered predictions repeatedly fail when the test coverage was adequate.
- Programme failure is an allowed outcome. If the record shows the programme losing ground, or a core empirical requirement below fails persistently, the programme must be labelled as such in this charter and in the README, not quietly continued.

Standing failure and constraint conditions, from the curated causal model and comparative evaluation:

- Shared insertions at the same location, with matching boundaries and shared derived disabling changes, strongly favour shared ancestry for those locations. The model must accept that history for those locations or supply an independently testable alternative.
- A persistent inability to define lineage boundaries independently prevents a genuine cross-lineage test.
- A need for unlimited placements or losses invented case by case counts against the model's explanatory adequacy.

## 7. Evaluation commitments

- Distinguish, in every file: observation, demonstrated mechanism, historical reconstruction, and starting premise.
- Charge every model the same cost for its extra assumptions.
- Preserve failed searches, negative cases, and provenance.
- Report chronologies and dates only together with the measurements and rate assumptions behind them.
- Keep current claims separate from earlier drafts. Earlier drafts live in `research/history/` and do not state the programme's current position.
