# Programme Charter: ERV Design Model

**Established:** 2026-11-18
**Framework:** Designed Functional Maturity (DFM)
**Structure:** Lakatosian scientific research programme
**Status:** Exploratory. Preliminary plausibility only. No parity, superiority, or historical confirmation claimed.

Human-Curated, AI-Enabled (HCAE)

## 1. Purpose

To develop and test, in public and under dated, preregistered commitments, whether a design model of ERV and ERV-like genomic evidence can become a progressive research programme, and to record honestly if it does not.

## 2. Hard core

The hard core is the programme's organizing commitment. It is held as hypothesis and presupposition, not as experimentally established fact:

1. Designed Functional Maturity: the initial biosphere was functionally mature, with working genomic, organismal, and ecological systems.
2. A potential original biological network: initially functional biological information handling, potentially including beneficial viral or virus-like delivery systems and functional genomic architecture.
3. Candidate original lineages: there were original lineages whose boundaries, if the programme is to be tested empirically, must be specified independently of the ERV patterns under test.

Theological premises inside these commitments are premises. They must never be reported as measurements, sequence findings, or conclusions of a genomic analysis.

## 3. Protective belt

The following are revisable. Revising them is a normal problemshift, not a programme failure, provided the revision is recorded, dated, and charged as an auxiliary change:

- Causal models of inheritance, ERV integration, and viral transmission.
- Models of co-option and functional recruitment after integration.
- Regulatory architecture and functional genomic module proposals.
- Genomic modification, silencing, degradation, and loss models.
- Candidate lineage (kind) boundaries and host compatibility rules, when independently specified.
- Integration targeting, host range, and timing proposals.

No protective belt proposal may be assigned arbitrarily to every observed locus. An initial placement, loss, or transfer invoked for a specific locus requires an independently stated expectation or locus specific evidence.

## 4. Positive heuristic

Work in this order, prospectively:

1. Comparative genomics at preselected loci, with assembly aware orthology checks: both flanks, insertion boundaries, target site duplications, LTR structure, and shared derived changes.
2. Independent lineage constraints: specify lineage boundaries and module expectations before candidate locus selection.
3. Prospective locus tests: use the preregistration template in `predictions/`; commit the prediction, disconfirmer, and scoring rule before inspecting the held outcome.
4. Functional perturbation evidence: weigh demonstrated causal function separately from historical origin; present function does not date origin.
5. Quantitative competing model comparisons: compare against the strongest common descent and exaptation models, with auxiliary burden charged symmetrically to every model. No numerical ranking without calibrated priors, sampling, and likelihood functions.

Predeclared test families from the curated comparative evaluation (T1 to T5) are the starting set: initial module specification, exact locus and phylogenetic congruence, nonorthologous transfer histories, symmetric cross-lineage A/B screening, and functional system integration.

## 5. Negative heuristic

- Do not direct anomalies at the hard core as a first move; test protective belt models first, in the open.
- Do not rescue a failed prediction with ad hoc initial placement, unrestricted loss, or undocumented transfer. A rescue that is not independently testable and preregistered for a new test is a degenerating move and must be logged as one.
- Do not misrepresent evidence: do not present family similarity as orthologous insertion, an assembly gap as absence, an unverified supplement as inspected data, or a premise as a finding.

## 6. Progressive versus degenerating problemshift

The programme is assessed on a dated record, in `predictions/result-log.md` and `governance/change-history.md`:

- Progressive: a revision predicts a novel fact, preregistered before the test, and at least some such predictions are corroborated under the stated scoring rule.
- Degenerating: revisions only accommodate facts already known, multiply untested auxiliaries, or repeatedly fail preregistered tests under adequate coverage.
- Programme failure is an allowed outcome. If the record becomes degenerating, or a core empirical requirement below fails persistently, the programme must be classified as such in the charter status and the README, not quietly continued.

Standing failure and constraint conditions, from the curated causal model and comparative evaluation:

- Shared orthologous insertions with matching boundaries and shared derived disabling mutations strongly favour shared ancestry for those loci. The model must accept that history for those loci or supply an independently testable alternative.
- Persistent inability to define lineage boundaries independently prevents a genuinely cross-kind test.
- A need for unlimited ad hoc initial placements or losses counts against explanatory adequacy.

## 7. Evaluation commitments

- Distinguish, in every file: observation, demonstrated mechanism, historical reconstruction, and initial condition premise.
- Charge auxiliary burden to all models on the same rules.
- Preserve failed searches, negative cases, and provenance.
- Report chronologies and dates only with their measurement and rate assumptions.
- Separate current claims from historical revisions. Historical files live in `research/history/` and do not state the programme's current position.
