# Glossary

Plain definitions for the terms used in this repository. One sentence each; the papers use the terms in these senses.

## Genome terms

- **Endogenous retrovirus (ERV):** the remains of an ancient retrovirus infection that became a permanent part of a species' genome and is inherited like any other DNA.
- **Retrovirus:** a virus that copies its RNA genome into DNA and inserts that DNA into the host cell's genome.
- **Provirus:** the inserted viral DNA copy while it sits in the host genome, before or after it becomes a permanent inherited element.
- **Long terminal repeat (LTR):** a repeated sequence found at both ends of an inserted retrovirus copy, which helps identify the insertion and its boundaries.
- **Orthologous insertion:** the same viral insertion found at the same genomic location in two species, the pattern most naturally explained by inheritance from a shared ancestor.
- **Target site duplication:** a short stretch of host DNA duplicated on both sides of an insertion at the moment it integrated, a fingerprint of a real insertion event.
- **Co-option:** the later recruitment of an inserted viral sequence for a useful function in the host.
- **Exaptation:** a feature that evolved or was present for one purpose being used for a different purpose, the standard term for co-option in evolutionary writing.
- **Common descent:** the standard explanation that species share ancestors, so shared genomic patterns are inherited from those ancestors.
- **Shared derived change:** a mutation or disabling change found inside the same insertion in two species, which is evidence about whether the insertion predates their split.

## Programme terms

- **Core commitments (hard core):** what the programme assumes at the start and does not test directly.
- **Working models (protective belt):** the revisable parts of the model that meet the evidence first and are expected to change.
- **Research plan (positive heuristic):** the programme's standing instructions for what to work on next.
- **Rules against special pleading (negative heuristic):** the programme's standing instructions for what may not be done to protect the core commitments, such as inventing an untestable rescue for a failed prediction.
- **Gaining ground (progressive):** a change to the model that predicts something new, registered in advance, and is borne out.
- **Losing ground (degenerating):** a pattern of changes that only explain what is already known, add untested assumptions, or keep failing registered predictions.
- **Preregistration:** writing down a prediction, what would count against it, and how it will be scored, dated and committed before the outcome data is examined.
- **Auxiliary assumption:** an extra assumption a model adds beyond its core claims, counted against it when models are compared.
